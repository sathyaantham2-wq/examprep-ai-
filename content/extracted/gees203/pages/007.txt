Harṣha attempted to extend his empire 
southward beyond the Narmada, but 
was held in check by Pulakeśhin II 
of the powerful Chālukya dynasty, 
whom we will meet soon. Harṣha 
also dreamed of uniting northern 
India, but achieved only mixed 
success. Although he formed an 
alliance with the Varman dynasty 
of Kāmarūpa (in today’s Assam; their 
capital Prāgjyotiṣha was close 
to today’s Guwahati), conflicts 
with neighbouring kingdoms 
continued. 
A Tripartite Struggle for Kannauj
After Harṣha’s death in 647, north India saw much political 
turmoil. Kannauj became the centre of a long, indecisive 
‘Tripartite Struggle’, as it is often called. The three warring 
parties were the Pālas from the east, the Gurjara-Pratīhāras 
from the west, and the Rāṣhṭrakūṭas from the Deccan

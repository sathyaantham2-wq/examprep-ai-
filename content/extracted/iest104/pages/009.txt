Further progress in hunting and gathering was reflected in the 
making of smaller stone tools. The main types of stone tools included 
scrapers, borers, and points. These indicate improved efficiency in 
hunting by developing projectiles which were tipped with sharp 
points.
Fig. 4.10. Middle Palaeolithic tools
Later, humans invented the bow and arrow, as well as parallel-sided 
blade and microblade tools made from rocks with glassy texture (very 
sharp when freshly made). They also hunted small game animals. 
Moreover, they developed symbolic communication, decorated the 
walls of caves and rock shelters with paintings, and used pigments 
to decorate their bodies. In addition, they were the first to produce 
beads of stone, bone, and shell.

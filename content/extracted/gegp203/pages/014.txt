If both numbers are doubled, then both numbers get an extra factor of 
2 in their prime factors. This 2 will be included as a factor in the largest 
common subpart, and so the HCF will double. For example, consider the 
numbers 270 and 50.
270 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 5
50 = 2 × 5 × 5
HCF = 2 × 5 = 10
Let us double these numbers to get 540 and 100.
540 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 5
100 = 2 × 2 × 5 × 5
HCF = 2 × 2 × 5 =20.
Consider the following two multiples of 14 — 14 × 6, 14 × 9. What is 
their HCF?
Clearly, 14 is a common factor. Is it also the highest common factor? 
To see it, let us calculate the prime factorisations.
14 × 6 = 2 × 7 × 2 × 3
14
14 × 9 = 2 × 7 × 3 × 3
14
HCF = 14 × 3 = 42.

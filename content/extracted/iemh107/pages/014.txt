7.4 Tree diagrams
A tree diagram is a visual representation used to list all possible 
outcomes of a multi-step experiment. A multi-step experiment involves 
a series of independent trials. For example, tossing a coin two times, or 
rolling a dice three times are examples of multi-step experiments. Each 
branch of the tree represents a possible outcome, and branches split to 
show different paths for subsequent events.
A tree diagram is useful for
• Visualising multi-step experiments, where each path from start to 
end represents one complete outcome.
• Listing all outcomes of a sample space.
So far in this chapter, we have seen examples of single-step 
experiments. Let us look at an example of a multi-step experiment.

Despite the harsh conditions, Ladakh is home to unique 
wildlife like snow leopards, ibex, and Tibetan antelopes. The 
Ladakhi people lead a simple life. The region is known for its 
ancient monasteries and colourful festivals such as Losar and

The Gangetic Plains 
As we move southwards from the Himalayas, we reach the 
vast and fertile Gangetic Plains. These plains have been an 
important part of the history and civilisation of India. These 
plains are nourished by mighty rivers originating from the 
Himalayas, providing a vital lifeline: water. The Ganga, Indus, 
and Brahmaputra river systems, along with their extensive 
network of tributaries, enrich the soil with minerals, making the 
region highly fertile and ideal for agriculture. The rivers bring 
with them minerals that enrich the soil, enabling abundant 
agriculture. The rivers are also a source for generating electricity. 
A large proportion of India’s population lives in these plains.

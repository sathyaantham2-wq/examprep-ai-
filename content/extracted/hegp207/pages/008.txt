Are the 4 triangles obtained by drawing the diagonals of a rectangle 
(regions 1 – 4 in the figure) of equal areas?
Clearly, the triangles 1 – 4 are not four 
congruent triangles. 
To compare their areas, let us consider any 
two adjacent triangles, say 1 and 2.
Since the area of a triangle depends on its 
height and the base, let us consider suitable 
height-base pairs in each of the triangles. If OD 
and OB are taken as the bases, then we see that 
both triangles have the same altitude!
We also have OB = OD, since the diagonals of 
a rectangle bisect each other. So, the triangles 1 and 2 have equal areas. 
Arguing this way, we can show that all four triangles have equal areas.
From this problem, we can make the 
following general statement.
In a triangle, the line joining a vertex to 
the midpoint of its opposite side divides 
the triangle into two triangles of equal 
areas.

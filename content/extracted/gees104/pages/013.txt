Many lateral roads also connected with other parts of India, 
especially the important ports on the western and eastern 
coasts, which were vibrant centres of trade. In the eastern 
region, major cities emerged, such as Śhiśhupalgarh (today 
Sisupalgarh, part of Bhubaneswar), which was the capital 
of the Kalinga region and followed a strict square ground 
plan, with imposing fortifications and broad streets. 
In the Subcontinent’s southern regions, cities began 
emerging from about 400 BCE, although recent excavations 
claim to find some signs of commercial activities 
going further back. Around this time, three kingdoms 
emerged—the Cholas, the Cheras and the Pānḍyas.

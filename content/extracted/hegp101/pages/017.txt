 A number obtained by multiplying a number by itself is called 
a square number. Squares of natural numbers are called 
perfect squares.
 All perfect squares end with 0, 1, 4, 5, 6 or 9. Squares can only 
have an even number of zeros at the end.
 Square root is the inverse operation of square. Every perfect 
square has two integral square roots. The positive square root 
of a number is denoted by the symbol √ . For example, √9 = 3.
 A number obtained by multiplying a number by itself three 
times is called a cube. For example 1, 8, 27, ... ,etc., are cubes.
 A number is a perfect square if its prime factors can be split 
into two identical groups.
 A number is a perfect cube if its prime factors can be split into 
three identical groups
 The symbol 3 denotes cube root. For example, 27 3 = 3.

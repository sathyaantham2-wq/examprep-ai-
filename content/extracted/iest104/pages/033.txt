4 – Early Humans

Civilisation
Before we move on...
Æ Early human history refers to the long period of human history 
before the invention of writing and is studied mainly through 
archaeological evidence such as tools, fossils, and cave art.
Æ Early humans evolved in Africa and gradually migrated to 
different parts of the world.
Æ During the Palaeolithic period, humans were hunter-gatherers 
who used stone tools like handaxes, cleavers, and scrapers, and 
lived in caves or open camps.
Æ In the Mesolithic period, humans developed microlithic tools 
and began occupying temporary settlements near rivers and 
lakes.
Æ The Neolithic period marked a major shift to agriculture and 
domestication of animals, leading to permanent villages, pottery 
making, and weaving.
Æ The Chalcolithic period witnessed the use of copper along with 
stone tools and the growth of early farming communities.
Æ These developments led to the emergence of Bronze Age 
civilisations, characterised by urban centres, trade, writing, 
administration, and social organisation.
Æ Major early world civilisations such as Sindhu–Sarasvatī, 
Mesopotamia, Egypt, and China developed independently 
in fertile river valleys and made significant contributions in 
agriculture, writing, architecture, administration, and culture.

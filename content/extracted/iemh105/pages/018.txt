Given: AFB is an arc. ∠ACB is the angle

D is a point on the circle outside arc 
AFB such that when we extend DC it

We shall again make use of the 
properties of isosceles triangles.
∠ACE = ∠ADC + ∠CAD = 2 ∠ADC, since CA = CD (and so ∠ADC = ∠CAD).
Similarly, ∠BCE = ∠BDC + ∠CBD = 2 ∠BDC.
Also, ∠ACB = ∠ACE – ∠BCE, and ∠ADB = ∠ADC – ∠BDC.
Hence ∠ACB = 2 ∠ADB.

Something very interesting comes 
out of this. This is what the activity 
before Theorem 9 suggested. Take 
an arc AB. Decide how you want to 
go from A to B along the arc. Take 
any point D on the circle, outside

Then, no matter where D is, so

the circle, ∠ADB is the same!

by AXB at the centre is the angle swept by CA as we move from CA to

3.6.2 The Magic of Cyclic Numbers
When we calculate the decimal for 1
7
 we get 0.142857142857 … or 
0.142857. The repeating block, 142857, is one of mathematics’ most 
fascinating gem —  a cyclic number. Watch what happens when we 
multiply it by the digits 1 through 6:
142857 × 1 = 142857
142857 × 2 = 285714
142857 × 3 = 428571
142857 × 4 = 571428
142857 × 5 = 714285
142857 × 6 = 857142
The same digits simply shift in a cyclic circle! This beautiful internal 
structure is a hallmark of the rational number 1
7 .
3.6.3 Irrational Decimals: Chaos and Infinity
Irrational numbers, however, possess decimal expansions that never 
end and never repeat. There is no cyclic block, no pattern that loops 
forever. Examples include:
2 = 1.4142135623730950488 …
π = 3.1415926535897932384 …

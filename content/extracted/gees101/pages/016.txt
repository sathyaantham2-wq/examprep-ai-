The Peninsular Plateau 
 LET’S REMEMBER
A plateau is a landform that rises up from the surrounding 
land and has a more or less flat surface; some of its sides are 
often steep slopes.
India has several plateaus; the most important one is the 
triangular peninsular area in the middle and south of the 
country. 
It is also a very old land formation! Since this region is a peninsula, 
surrounded by water on all three sides by the Arabian Sea, the Bay 
of Bengal, and the Indian Ocean, it is called a peninsular plateau. 
The plateau is bordered by two mountain ranges, the Western 
Ghats and Eastern Ghats. The Western Ghats are taller and run 
along the western coast like a wall, with many beautiful waterfalls 
flowing down their steep sides during the monsoon season.
 DON’T MISS OUT
The Western Ghats have been declared a UNESCO World 
Heritage Site. They are home to many rivers and have a rich 
biodiversity. The northern portion of the Western Ghats are 
also known as the Sahyadri Hills.
The Eastern Ghats are lower and broken into smaller hills 
along the eastern coast. Between these mountain ranges lies the 
Deccan Plateau, a vast area of flat highlands.
Rivers like the Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri flow across the 
plateau from west to east. These rivers are important for farming 
and provide water to millions of people.

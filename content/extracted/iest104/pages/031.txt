Some other craft items unique to the Chinese civilisation were the 
jade objects and figurines, which were either ritual or prestige 
objects. They probably played an important role in social transactions, 
as jade was not locally available and was obtained from outside 
China. Marble was also carved into ornaments in the form of birds 
and animals, and as foundations for wooden pillars. Besides these, 
the Chinese mastered bronze metallurgy to produce weapons, tools, 
and elaborate ritual vessels.

Chinese artisans skilfully carved jade into shapes of objects such 
as fish and tied them with a cord. When struck, these jade pieces 
produced a clear and lasting musical sound. This practice reflects 
the importance of music in Chinese culture and highlights the high 
level of craftsmanship achieved by the Chinese people.
The Great Wall of China was built over a period stretching to two 
thousand years. Initially, several walls were built from 680 BCE 
onwards by the Zhou and other dynasties as protection against the 
violent raids of nomadic tribes. These were later joined together to 
make an effective defence mechanism. The expansion and repair 
of the wall continued till the 17th century CE.

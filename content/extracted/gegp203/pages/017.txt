Finding Common Ground
Do you see that, in each case, the number by which the LCM is multiplied 
to get the product is actually the HCF?
Thus, our observations seem to suggest the following:
HCF × LCM = Product of the two numbers.
Why does this happen? Can you give an explanation or proof?
[Hint: Consider the prime factorisation of the given numbers. 
Among their prime factors, some are common to both factorisations, 
and the rest occur in only one of them. Between the HCF and the LCM, 
see how the common and non-common prime factors get distributed. 
In the product, observe how these two kinds of prime factors occur. 
Compare them.]
Explore whether this property holds when 3 numbers are considered.

how many cows did he have? (Based on the folklore mathematics 
from Karnataka.)

b. Constitutional monarchy
Let us consider an example of a different kind of monarchy. 
The United Kingdom or Britain is also a monarchy. The 
head of the state is the king or queen, but he or she has 
only nominal power and the real power of the executive 
is exercised by the prime minister. The legislative power is 
exercised by the parliament.
This means that all the laws are made by the elected 
parliament, and executive power is exercised by the Council 
of Ministers led by the prime minister. So while Britain has 
a king, it really is, today, a parliamentary democracy. This 
kind of monarchy is called a constitutional monarchy.

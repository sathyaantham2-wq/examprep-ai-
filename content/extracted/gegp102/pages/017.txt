The observations that we have made in the previous two examples
can be seen in a general way as follows.
Consider 10 x 98 + 3 x 98. This means taking the sum of 10 times 98
and 3 times 98.
(98 added 10 times) + (98 added 3 times)
Clearly, this is the same as 10 + 3 = 13 times 98. Thus,
10 x 98 + 3 x 98 = (10 + 3) x 98.
Writing this equality the other way, we get
(10 + 3) x 98 = 10 x 98 + 3 x 98.
Swapping the numbers in the products above, this property can be
seen in the following form:
98 x 10 + 98 x 3 = 98 x (10 + 3), and
98 x (10 + 3) = 98 x 10 + 98 x 3.

Similarly, let us consider the expression 14 x 10 - 6 x 10. This means
subtracting 6 times 10 from 14 times 10.
(10 added 14 times, minus 10 added 6 times)
Clearly, this is 14 - 6 = 8 times 10. Thus,
14 x 10 - 6 x 10 = (14 - 6) x 10,
or
(14 - 6) x 10 = 14 x 10 - 6 x 10
This property can be nicely summed up as follows:
The multiple of a sum (difference) is the same as the sum (difference)
of the multiples.

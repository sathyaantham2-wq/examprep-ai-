5. Imagine that the Harappan script gets deciphered tomorrow. 
What new types of information do you think historians might 
learn?
6. Prepare a table with three columns—Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, and 
Neolithic—and fill in their distinctive features: tools, settlements, 
art, and subsistence.
7. “Bronze Age civilisations developed independently but shared 
common features.” Examine this statement with reference to the 
civilisations given in the chapter.
8. Although rivers provided many benefits, they also created 
challenges for early societies. Discuss both the advantages and 
disadvantages of settling near rivers.
9. With the help of your teacher, find out more about the Code of 
Hammurabi. Why was it important? Do you think it was fair to all 
sections of society? Give reasons for your answer.
10. If you had to choose one major innovation from early civilisations 
that changed the world permanently, what would it be and why?
11. Compare the social hierarchy and daily life of people in the 
Egyptian civilisation with those in Mesopotamia or China. What 
similarities and differences do you notice?
12. Activity: Using maps, locate the major rivers and civilisations 
of Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, and the Sindhu–Sarasvatī Valley. 
Mark the trade links between them.
13. Activity: Choose one early civilisation (Mesopotamia, Egypt, or 
China) and prepare a mini-scrapbook or a presentation showing 
their innovations in tools, writing, art, and architecture. Include 
pictures, brief descriptions, and explain their significance.

Why does Baudhāyana’s method work?
Can you see why the method works in the case where the two squares 
are the same size? Does it agree with the method we used earlier to 
combine two same sized squares into a bigger square?
Subsequently in his Śulba-Sūtra (Verse 2.1), Baudhāyana provides 
another verse that helps in explaining why the method works in general:
To combine different squares, mark a rectangular portion of the 
larger square using a side of the smaller square. The diagonal of this 
rectangle is the side of a square that has area equal to the sum of the 
areas of the smaller squares.

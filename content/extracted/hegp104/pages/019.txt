Deduction 9— What can we say about the angles in a rhombus?
Consider a rhombus GAME.
In ∆GAE, since GE = GA, a = d.
Similarly, in ∆MAE, since ME = MA, 
b = c. 
It can be seen that ∆GAE ≅ ∆MAE 
(How?)
So, a = b, c = d and ∠G = ∠M (since 
they are corresponding parts of congruent triangles).
Thus, we have, a = b = c = d.
These facts hold for any rhombus. Let us apply them to the rhombus 
ABCD that we constructed earlier. Let the four equal angles formed by 
the diagonal be a, as shown in the figure
In ∆ADB, we have
a + a + 50 = 180°.
So, a = 65°.
Thus, the angles of the rhombus ABCD 
are 50°, 130°, 50°, and 130°.
So, in a rhombus opposite angles are 
equal to each other.
Interestingly, there is one more way by 
which we could have figured out the other 
angles of the rhombus ABCD. We have 
shown that in a general rhombus GAME, the four angles formed by a 
diagonal are equal to each other.

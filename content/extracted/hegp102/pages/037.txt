9. A dairy plans to produce 8.5 billion packets of milk in a year. They 
want a unique ID (identifier) code for each packet. If they choose to 
use the digits 0–9, how many digits should the code consist of?
Ans: Total packets of milk in a year = 8,500,000,000 
Total digits (0-9) = 10, Let each code have ‘n’ digits 
So, number of unique ID codes possible = 10n
So, 10n
 ≥ 8,500,000,000 
But, 109
 < 8,500,000,000 and 1010 > 8,500,000,000. So, n = 10
Hence at least 10 digits are required in the code.

11. A digital locker has an alphanumeric (it can have both digits and 
letters) passcode of length 5. Some example codes are G89P0, 38098, 
BRJKW, and 003AZ. How many such codes are possible?
Ans: We have 26 letters and 10 digits (0-9) there are 36 possible characters 
for each of the length of 5. 
Total possible codes are 36 × 36 × 36 × 36 × 36 = 365
.

In the Himalayan region, water flowing downhill converts its 
potential energy into kinetic energy. Traditionally, this energy 
was used in devices, such as the gharat or panchakki — a water 
mill used to grind grain (Fig. 7.36). These can still be found in 
hilly regions. 
The water starts from the top with potential energy. This 
potential energy gets converted to kinetic energy as it comes 
down the pipe (A). The kinetic energy of the water drives 
the wheel (B) and sets it into rotational motion. The wheel is 
connected to the grinding stone at the top (C). 
In modern times, the potential energy of the water stored 
in dams is similarly converted into kinetic energy to generate 
electricity.
At a Glance
y Work is done by a force on an object, when the force displaces the 
object in the direction of the force.
y An object having capacity to do work is said to possess energy.
y Work-energy theorem: Work done on an object or a system, is 
equal to the change in its energy. 
y The energy possessed by an object due to its motion is called 
kinetic energy.
y The energy stored by an object as a result of its deformation or in 
a system of objects due to their relative positions is called potential 
energy.
y Power is defined as the rate at which work is done.
y Simple machines are devices that make work easier by changing 
the magnitude or direction of the force that needs to be applied, 
though they do not reduce total work.

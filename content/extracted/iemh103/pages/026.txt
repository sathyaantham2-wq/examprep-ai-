Chapter Summary
In this chapter you have learnt the following concepts:
• Natural Numbers () are the counting numbers 
{1, 2, 3, … } that emerged at least tens of thousands of years ago 
due to humanity’s need to count.
• The Concept of Zero (Śhūnya) was formalised in India by 
philosophers and then brought into mathematics formally by 
Brahmagupta (629 CE), who transformed the philosophical state 
of ‘nothingness’ (Śhūnyatā) into an actual number on which 
one could perform arithmetic operations. Brahmagupta also 
introduced the negative numbers, i.e., numbers less than zero.
• Integers (Z) extend the number line to the left of 1 to include zero 
as well as the negative numbers, which Brahmagupta historically 
categorised as ‘debts’ (ṛiṇa) in contrast with the positive number 
‘fortunes’ (dhana).

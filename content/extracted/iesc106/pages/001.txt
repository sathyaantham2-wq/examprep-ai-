In Chapter 4, you learnt to describe the motion of an object in terms 
of its position, velocity and acceleration. But you did not consider 
what causes motion. Is there an underlying cause for a change in 
position and velocity of an object? What is the nature of this cause? 
Do all motions require a cause? In this chapter, we will investigate 
what causes changes in the motion of objects. We will also discuss 
Newton’s three laws of motion and learn how to apply them.
6.1 The Concept of Force
You may recall learning earlier that a force can make an 
object move from rest, change the speed and direction 
of motion of a moving object, and can even change the 
shape of an object. For example, a ball at rest starts moving when 
you apply a force, a force applied by a cricket bat on a cricket ball 
changes its direction, and a lemon can be squeezed by the force 
applied by your fingers (Fig. 6.1).

The Sātavāhanas 
From the limited evidence available, the Śhungas seem to have 
waged wars with many of their neighbouring regions. This 
may have included the Sātavāhanas, who ruled large parts of 
the Deccan from the 2nd century BCE onward, to the south of 
the Śhunga Empire. Sometimes referred to as ‘Andhras’, the 
Sātavāhanas were a powerful dynasty and their empire largely 
comprised of present-day Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and 
Maharashtra, with different capital cities at different times — the 
most famous were Amrāvatī and Pratiṣhṭhāna (Paithan). Trade 
and commerce appear to have flourished in the Sātavāhana 
kingdom.

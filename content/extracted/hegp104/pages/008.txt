The Carpenter’s Problem shows that rectangles can also be defined as 
follows—
Rectangle: A rectangle is a quadrilateral whose diagonals are equal 
and bisect each other.
Observe how different this definition is from the earlier one. Yet, both 
capture the same class of quadrilaterals. Further, it turns out that the 
first definition can be simplified.
In the earlier definition, we stated that a rectangle has (a) opposite sides 
of equal length, and (b) all angles equal to 90°. Would we be wrong if we 
just define a rectangle as a quadrilateral in which all the angles are 90°?
If you think that this definition is incomplete, try constructing a 
quadrilateral in which the angles are all 90° but the opposite sides are 
not equal. 
Are you able to construct such a quadrilateral? 
Let us prove why this is impossible.

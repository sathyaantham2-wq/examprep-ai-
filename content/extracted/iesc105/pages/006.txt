5.3 Methods of Separation of Homogeneous Mixtures
5.3.1 Crystallization
You have learnt to prepare a saturated solution. If you take a saturated 
solution of compound ‘B’ in water at 60 °C, prepared by dissolving 287 g 
of compound ‘B’ in 100 g of water, what will happen if you gradually cool 
it to 40 °C? As per Fig. 5.6, the solubility of compound ‘B’ is 241 g per 100 g 
of water at 40 °C. It implies that only 241 g of compound ‘B’ can remain 
in the solution and the rest of it will separate out as a pure solid, often 
as crystals. A crystal is a solid that is made up of particles arranged in a 
regular geometric pattern.

The circumference of a circle 
of diameter d is π d. Since the 
diameter is twice the radius, we can 
also write the circumference of a 
circle as 2πr, where r is the radius. 
What will be the length of a 
semicircle with the same radius r 
(see Fig. 6.8)?
If we reflect the circle in the 
diameter AB, the red semicircle 
and the blue semicircle exchange 
places. This means that they have 
equal length, which is 2πr ÷ 2 = πr.
Instead of reflecting the figure in the diameter AB, we can also 
imagine rotating the whole figure around the centre O through an 
angle of 180°, i.e., through a half-turn. The effect is the same, and we 
get the same formula for the length of the semicircular arc.

Tibetan Refugees in India
Located on the other side of the Himalayan range, Tibet is India’s 
northern neighbour. In the 7th century, the ‘Land of Snows’, as 
Tibet is sometimes called, became a centre of Buddhism when 
its king embraced the Buddha’s teachings. Gradually, despite a 
few setbacks, Buddhism expanded in Tibet. 
During the 12th and 13th centuries, Turko-Afghan military 
invasions in eastern India led to the destruction of Nālandā and 
other Buddhist centres of learning (see ‘Turning Tides: 11th and 
12th Centuries’ in this textbook). Many monks left for Tibet, 
often taking precious manuscripts of Sanskrit texts with them.

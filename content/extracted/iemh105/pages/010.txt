Let us see why this is true. Draw a 
circle with centre C (Fig. 5.12). Draw 
any chord AB. Let M be the midpoint 
of AB. We must show that CM is 
perpendicular to AB.
Explanation: CAB is an isosceles 
triangle. AB is the base and CA = CB. 
So, ∠A = ∠B. 
M is the midpoint of AB. So, 
AM = BM. 
By the SAS congruence, ∆ CMA is 
congruent to ∆ CMB. So, ∠CMB = ∠CMA.
But ∠CMB + ∠CMA = 180° (angles on 
a line).
So, both angles are 90°. That is, CM 
is perpendicular to AB.

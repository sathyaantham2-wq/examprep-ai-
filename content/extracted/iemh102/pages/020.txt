We see that,
y = 2x + 5 cuts the y-axis at A (0, 5),
y = x + 3 cuts the y-axis at B (0, 3), and
y = 3x – 2 cuts the y-axis at C (0, –2).
We observe that any straight line written in the form y = ax + b cuts 
the y-axis at the point (0, b). The length b is referred to as the y-intercept
of the line. Thus, the y-intercept of the line y = x + 3 is 3. This means that 
the line cuts the y-axis at a distance of 3 units from the origin in the 
positive direction. Similarly, the y-intercept of the line y = 3x – 2 is – 2.

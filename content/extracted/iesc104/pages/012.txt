Fig. 4.13: Position-time graph of a moving object 
As we can see in Fig. 4.13a, a straight line position-time graph indicates 
that the object is moving with a constant velocity. On the other hand, a 
curved position-time graph as in Fig. 4.13b, indicates that the velocity is not 
constant, and thus, the object is in accelerated motion.
Which physical quantities can be obtained from a position-time graph? 
From the position-time graph, you can find the position of an object at 
each instant of time. Does the graph provide information about any other 
physical quantity as well? In fact, you can also calculate how 
fast the position is changing, i.e., the magnitude of velocity of 
the object. How can you do that?

Sustainable pathways
In recent years, there has been a growing interest in combining 
the productivity of modern methods with the wisdom of 
traditional practices to create sustainable agriculture. This 
approach is inspired by Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS) and 
focuses on environment-friendly, long-lasting farming practices. 
One example is the resurgence of organic farming, which avoids 
the use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides. Instead, it relies 
on natural fertilisers, such as compost, seed cake (residue after 
oil has been extracted from seeds) and manure from animal 
sources. Another example is the use of neem-based pesticides, 
which are natural and much less harmful to the environment 
than chemical ones.
 DON’T MISS OUT
Sikkim is the world’s first 100 per cent organic state. It 
adopted an organic farming model for sustainable agriculture 
by banning in 2014 chemical fertilisers and pesticides, and 
focusing on natural farming practices. (We visited Sikkim in

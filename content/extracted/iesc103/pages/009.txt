The everyday experiences mentioned above are related to blood and its 
components. 
1. The red colour of blood is due to haemoglobin, an iron-rich protein 
in the Red Blood Cells (RBCs). RBCs live for about 4 months and are 
replaced regularly.
2. Platelets help in blood clotting at the site of the injury.
3. During exercise or running, muscles need more oxygen, so breathing 
becomes faster and blood flow increases (face appears red).
4. White Blood Cells (WBCs) collect at infected areas, causing pus 
formation and inflammation (causing redness and swelling, and 
possible pus formation at the infected area).

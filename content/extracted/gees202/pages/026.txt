The Indonesian archipelago
India and Indonesia, separated by the Bay of Bengal and the 
Andaman Sea, are close maritime neighbours with a long history 
of cultural, religious and commercial exchange. Indonesia is 
not a single landmass: it is actually an archipelago, that is, an 
extensive group of islands (Fig. 2.20) — in this case, several large 
islands and over 17,000 smaller ones! The two neighbouring 
nations have a legacy of trade 
and sociocultural interaction that 
dates back to early maritime trade 
between Indian kingdoms and the 
large islands of Java and Sumatra 
over 2,000 years ago. Collaborations 
between India’s ancient university of 
Nālandā and the ancient Indonesian 
Muara Jambi temple complex 
further strengthened the bond. 
Later, Islam travelled to Indonesia 
from the shores of India.

The Big Questions
If you had to choose, would you spend your pocket money on snacks 
or save it for a new pair of shoes? If your school library had only five 
copies of a new storybook but 20 students wanted to read it, who 
should get the book first? Can the students share them or should the 
school buy more copies? Which type of crop should a farmer grow 
based on the condition of the soil, rainfall, and market demand? 
Should an enterprise employ more labour or capital in the production 
process? Should the government spend more on building highways 
or hospitals? 
These are not random questions; rather, these are examples of 
economic choices that individuals, enterprises, and governments 
have to make. 
Some of our preferences are needs, such as essentials like food, water, 
and shelter, while others are wants, like gadgets, vacations, or luxury 
items. Human wants are unlimited and keep changing; for instance, 
people may want to upgrade from a bicycle to a motorbike and then 
to a car.

[p.106-107] 5.1 Across the Line
Fold square paper, draw lines on creases. When a pair of lines meet at a point on a plane surface, they intersect. Line l intersects line m: 4 angles formed (a,b,c,d).
If ∠a=120°: ∠a,∠b linear pair (adjacent, on a straight line) sum to 180° so ∠b=60°. ∠b,∠c linear pair so ∠c=120°. ∠c,∠d linear pair so ∠d=60°. So ∠a=∠c=120°, ∠b=∠d=60° (vertically opposite angles equal).
General proof: ∠a+∠b=180°, ∠a+∠d=180° => ∠b=∠d. ∠b+∠a=180°,∠b+∠c=180° => ∠a=∠c.
Linear pairs (adjacent angles on a straight line) always add to 180°. Opposite angles (vertically opposite) are always equal - this reasoning/justification is called a proof.

[p.108] Measurement errors: improper protractor use, line thickness (ideal lines have no thickness). Geometry uses ideal versions, reasoning not measurement gives exact 180°.

5.2 Perpendicular Lines
If two intersecting lines form 4 equal angles, each must be 90° (right angle). Perpendicular lines intersect at right angles (90°).

[p.109-110] 5.3 Between Lines
Line segments can meet at endpoint, cross at interior point, or not meet. Example: FG,FH meet at endpoint F at angle 115.3°.
Parallel lines: pair of lines on same plane that do not meet however far extended (both directions). Must lie on same plane (a line on table + line on board don't make them parallel even if they never meet).

[p.111] 5.4 Parallel and Perpendicular Lines in Paper Folding
Square sheet: opposite edges parallel; adjacent edges perpendicular (meet at point, form right angles).
Fold horizontally in half: 3 parallel lines appear (2 original edges + 1 new crease), new line perpendicular to vertical sides.
Fold again: 5 parallel lines. Fold again: 9 parallel lines. Pattern: 2^n + 1 parallel lines after n horizontal folds.
Vertical fold: new vertical line perpendicular to the horizontal lines.
Diagonal fold: can find fold parallel to diagonal.

[p.112] Triangle corner-fold activity: line segments a,b,c parallel to p,q,r respectively (a∥p since same relation to crease; b∥q both perpendicular to same lines; c∥r both parallel to same diagonal).

Notations: single arrow (>) marks one set of parallel lines; double arrow marks second set; square symbol marks 90° perpendicular angle.

[p.113] Figure It Out:
1. Draw lines perpendicular to given lines on dot paper.
2. Mark parallel lines (arrow notation) and perpendicular lines (square symbol) in a complex figure. (a) Perpendicular lines spotted via right-angle corners on grid (b) Parallel lines spotted via constant distance apart / same direction on grid.
3. Draw sets of parallel lines on dot paper with dot endpoints.

[p.114] 4. Draw lines parallel to given segments on dot paper (some easy - horizontal/vertical/45°; some harder - other orientations, done using equidistance intuition).
5. Fig 5.13: which line (b or c) is parallel to line a? Answer: line c (line c stays same distance from a throughout; line b does not).
Note: parallel lines only meaningful on same plane; transversals help determine parallelism precisely.

[p.115] 5.5 Transversals
Line t crosses lines l and m: called a transversal. 8 angles formed (1-8), with 4 pairs of vertically opposite angles (∠1=∠3, ∠2=∠4, ∠5=∠7, ∠6=∠8), so maximum 4 distinct angle measures possible among the 8 (not all 8 can differ; also not 5 distinct values since e.g. ∠2=∠4 always).

5.6 Corresponding Angles
Angles in same relative position at each intersection are corresponding angles: ∠1&∠5, ∠2&∠6, ∠3&∠7, ∠4&∠8.

[p.116] Activity 3 (constructing parallel lines via equal corresponding angles): Draw line l, transversal t meeting at X, ∠a=60° (so linear pair angle=120°, two distinct angles present: 60°,120°). Mark point Y on t, draw line m through Y making 60° angle with t (∠b=60°, corresponding to ∠a). Lines l,m appear parallel.

[p.117] Rule: When corresponding angles formed by a transversal on a pair of lines are equal, the lines are parallel.
Activity 4: parallel lines l,m with transversal t, ∠a,∠b corresponding - tracing paper confirms equal. Corresponding angles formed by transversal on parallel lines are ALWAYS equal (converse also true - necessary and sufficient condition).
Activity 5: hard to draw transversal on non-parallel-looking lines l,m such that corresponding angles equal, because unless lines are actually parallel, no transversal can make corresponding angles equal.

[p.118] When lines are NOT parallel, corresponding angles formed by any transversal can never be equal.

5.7 Drawing Parallel Lines
Using set square + ruler: slide set square along ruler to draw multiple lines perpendicular to line l. These lines are parallel to each other (each makes 90° corresponding angle with l as transversal, corresponding angles equal => lines parallel).
Similarly draw parallel lines using the long (slanted) side of set square - corresponding angles equal by construction.

[p.119] Figure It Out: Draw line through point A parallel to line l using geometry box tools.
Method: Place set square with one edge along l; hold ruler against the other edge (ruler won't move); slide set square along ruler until edge reaches A; draw line along that edge through A. This new line is parallel to l (corresponding angles both = same set-square angle).

Making Parallel Lines through Paper Folding: For line l (crease) and point A: fold perpendicular to l through A (call it t); fold perpendicular to t through A again (call it m). Lines l and m are parallel (both perpendicular to t, so corresponding angles = 90° each, equal => parallel).

[p.120] Why parallel? Because l⊥t and m⊥t means corresponding angles at both intersections with transversal t are both 90°, hence equal, hence l∥m.

5.8 Alternate Angles
∠d is the alternate angle of ∠f (found via: corresponding angle of ∠f is ∠b, then vertically opposite of ∠b is ∠d). ∠c is alternate angle of ∠e.
Activity 6: If ∠f=120°: ∠b (corresponding to ∠f)=120°; ∠d (vertically opposite ∠b)=120°. So ∠f=∠d always (irrespective of measure), proven via corresponding+vertically-opposite reasoning (a proof, not measurement).
Alternate angles formed by transversal on parallel lines are always equal.

[p.120-121] Example 1: parallel lines l,m, transversal t. ∠6=135°. Then ∠2=135° (corresponding to ∠6). ∠8=135° (vert. opp. to ∠6). ∠4=135° (corresponding to ∠8, or vert.opp to ∠2). So ∠2,∠4,∠6,∠8=135°. ∠5 (linear pair with ∠6)=180-135=45°. Similarly ∠1,∠3,∠7=45°.

Example 2: lines l,m, transversal t. ∠a=120°, ∠f=70°. ∠b (linear pair with ∠a)=60°. ∠b is corresponding angle of ∠f. If parallel, ∠b should=∠f, but 60°≠70°. So lines l,m are NOT parallel.

[p.121-122] Example 3: parallel lines l,m, transversal t, ∠3=50°. ∠2 (linear pair with ∠3)=130°. ∠2,∠6 corresponding (parallel lines) so ∠6=130°.
∠3,∠6 are called interior angles (between the two lines, same side of transversal... actually co-interior/consecutive interior). Sum of interior angles on same side of transversal = 180° (∠3+∠6=50+130=180°, always true for parallel lines - "co-interior angles" / "allied angles" property, i.e. sum to 180°).

Example 4: AB∥CD, AD∥BC (parallelogram ABCD... actually quadrilateral with A top-left,B top-right,D bottom-left,C bottom-right forming rhombus-like shape). ∠DAC=65°, ∠ADC=60°. AD is transversal of AB∥CD: ∠ADC+∠DAB=180° (co-interior) => ∠DAB=120°. ∠DAB=∠DAC+∠CAB => 120=65+∠CAB => ∠CAB=55°.
CD is transversal of AD∥BC: ∠ADC+∠BCD=180° => ∠BCD=120°.
Similarly ∠ABC=60° (co-interior with ∠DAB via transversal AB... by symmetry).
Answers: ∠CAB=55°, ∠ABC=60°, ∠BCD=120°.

[p.125] 5.9 Parallel Illusions: radial/houndstooth patterns that create illusion of non-parallel lines that are actually parallel or vice versa - due to visual context/surrounding angles distorting perception (Zöllner-type illusion).

[p.126] SUMMARY:
- Two intersecting lines form 4 angles; vertically opposite angles equal; linear pairs sum to 180°.
- If all 4 angles formed are 90° (equal), lines are perpendicular.
- Lines that never intersect on a plane are parallel.
- A line t intersecting a pair of lines is a transversal; forms 2 sets of 4 angles, each angle in first set has a corresponding angle in second set.
- Transversal on parallel lines: corresponding angles equal. Converse: if corresponding angles equal, lines are parallel.
- Transversal on parallel lines: alternate angles equal.
- Interior angles on same side of transversal cutting parallel lines sum to 180° (co-interior/allied angles).

Deltas
A delta is a landform formed at the mouth of a river, where it flows 
into a sea, ocean, or lake, and deposits the sediments it has carried 
from upstream. Over time, these deposits accumulate to form a 
fan-shaped or triangular area of land. Deltas are highly fertile due 
to their rich alluvial soil, making them ideal for agriculture and 
supporting crops, such as rice and jute. They are also important for 
fishing, as the mix of fresh and saltwater creates diverse aquatic life. 
Many deltas support dense human settlements and are centres of 
trade and transportation because rivers provide navigable routes. 
However, they can also be prone to flooding, which affects human 
life and activities.

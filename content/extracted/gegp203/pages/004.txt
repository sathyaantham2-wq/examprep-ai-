Factors of a Number Using Prime Factorisation
From the prime factorisation of a number, we can construct all its factors. 
This can be used to simplify the procedure for finding the HCF of two 
numbers.
Consider the number 840 and its prime factorisation 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 × 7.
Is 2 × 2 × 7 = 28 a factor of 840?
If yes, what should it be multiplied by to get 840?
To answer these questions, we can reorder the prime factors of 840 
as follows (recall that reordering factors does not change the product):
840 = (2 × 2 × 7) × (2 × 3 × 5).
So,
840 = 28 × 30.
Thus, (2 × 2 × 7) = 28 is a factor of 840, and it should be multiplied by 
30 (2 × 3 × 5) to get 840.
